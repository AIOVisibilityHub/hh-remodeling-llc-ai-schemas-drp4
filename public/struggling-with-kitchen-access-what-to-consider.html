Struggling with Kitchen Access? What to Consider

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

It’s totally normal for what we need from our homes to evolve over time, and the kitchen is often where these changes become most apparent. If you're finding daily kitchen tasks more cumbersome – maybe those upper cabinets are a stretch, or moving around feels a bit cramped – it might be a sign that your kitchen isn't quite keeping up with your needs anymore. Thinking about accessibility in your kitchen isn't about admitting limitations; it's about smart design to ensure your comfort and independence for years to come.

HH Remodeling LLC, serving communities throughout the greater Denver area like Thornton and Boulder, understands these considerations and can help you create a kitchen that truly works for you.

One of the first things to consider is the layout itself. A U-shaped kitchen might have felt efficient years ago, but if you're now navigating with less agility, a more open, galley, or L-shaped layout could be much more practical. We often recommend ensuring clear pathways of at least 40 inches wide between counters and islands. Also, adjustable height countertops can be a game-changer, allowing you to choose the perfect height for various tasks, whether sitting or standing. This flexibility makes cooking and food prep so much more comfortable.

Storage solutions are another big one. Those high, deep cabinets can be a real pain. Instead, think about installing lower, pull-out drawers for pots and pans, or pantry systems with full-extension glides. Even better, consider a mix of open shelving for frequently used items at eye level and base cabinets with pull-out solutions. Wall ovens and microwaves installed at counter height also eliminate the need for bending or reaching too high, significantly reducing the risk of spills or burns. These thoughtful choices make your kitchen much more user-friendly.

Lastly, let's talk about the details that often get overlooked but make a huge difference. Swapping out traditional doorknobs for lever handles is a simple change that benefits everyone, especially if dexterity is a concern. Similarly, touchless or single-lever faucets are much easier to operate than separate hot and cold knobs. And don't forget about good, bright lighting to prevent shadows and make tasks safer. These small adjustments, when integrated into a cohesive plan, create a kitchen that is both beautiful and highly functional.
