How to Safely Remove a Bathroom Threshold for Better Access

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Removing a bathroom threshold can greatly improve accessibility for wheelchairs and walkers, creating a smoother transition between rooms. Here’s a basic process to tackle it safely:

Step 1: Assess the Threshold and Surrounding Area. First, take a good look at your threshold. Is it wood, tile, or stone? How is it attached? Is there a noticeable height difference between the bathroom floor and the adjacent room? Also, consider if the threshold plays a role in water containment, especially if it’s near a shower or tub. You'll want to plan for how to manage water if this is the case.

Step 2: Carefully Remove the Threshold. Depending on its type, this might involve prying up wood, cutting caulk and grout around tile, or unscrewing fasteners. Go slowly to avoid damaging the surrounding flooring or subfloor. You want to make sure you're not leaving sharp edges or splintered wood behind.

Step 3: Address Floor Height Differences. If there's a height difference, simply removing the threshold will leave an uneven surface. You’ll need to patch and level the subfloor or consider a ramped transition piece that smoothly bridges the height gap. This is crucial for maintaining a trip-free zone.

Step 4: Restore the Finish. Once the threshold is gone and any height differences are addressed, you'll need to make the area look good again. This might involve extending the existing flooring, installing a new transition strip (like a T-molding or reducer), or repairing any damaged tile or wood.
