Common Mistakes When Improving Driveway Access

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're looking to make your driveway-to-entry path safer and more accessible, it's easy to overlook a few key things that can compromise your efforts. Here are some common mistakes to avoid:

  • Leaving the approach cluttered: We've all done it—garden hoses, tools, or even kids' toys can end up near the path. But failing to keep the route clear of all obstructions is a major trip hazard. Make it a habit to clear the path regularly.
  • Failing to maintain traction: Especially in our Colorado weather, simply clearing snow isn't enough. Neglecting to use ice melt or sand, or not having non-slip surfaces, can lead to dangerous slips. Always have supplies handy and use them promptly.
  • Under-lighting the approach: One or two bright lights by the door might seem sufficient, but often, the path itself remains in shadow. You need even, consistent lighting from the driveway all the way to the door to highlight any changes in elevation or obstacles.
  • Not addressing minor settlement or edge differences: Those small cracks or slightly uneven slabs might not seem like a big deal now, but they can worsen over time and become significant trip hazards. Catching and fixing these minor issues early prevents bigger problems.
  • Ignoring proper ramp geometry and handrail placement: If you're adding a ramp or handrails, simply installing them isn't enough. The slope of the ramp, its surface traction, and the stability and height of handrails are critical for safety and often need to meet specific codes.
