What Are the Biggest Mistakes in Accessible Kitchen Layout?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're designing or remodeling a kitchen for accessibility, it’s easy to make some common blunders that can really hinder usability. Knowing what to avoid can save you a lot of headaches later on. One big mistake is putting heavy or frequently used items either too high or too low.

Think about it: constantly reaching way up or bending way down isn't practical or safe. Another frequent error is leaving cords or clutter in access paths. It might seem minor, but those can create tripping hazards and block clear movement, which is critical for safe appliance use.

Also, never assume an appliance is “reachable” without actually checking the turning room and safe approach space. What looks fine on paper might be tight in reality, especially if you’re using a mobility aid. Using chairs instead of proper step stools for reaching is another common mistake that can lead to falls.
