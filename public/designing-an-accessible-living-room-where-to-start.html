Designing an Accessible Living Room: Where to Start?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Creating an accessible living room isn't just about functionality; it's about comfort and independence. If you're considering modifications for mobility or just want a more universally friendly space in your Denver home, knowing where to begin can feel a bit daunting. As HH Remodeling LLC, we've guided many homeowners through this process, and it really comes down to a few key steps.

First, measure your room thoroughly. Don't just get the overall dimensions; mark down the location of all doorways, windows, and any built-in features. This detailed map is your blueprint.

Next, identify all circulation routes. Think about how people will move through the room to different areas—to the sofa, to a window, to another room. These pathways are crucial.

For a straight accessible path, you're generally looking at a minimum of 36 inches, though 42 inches or more is often much more comfortable, especially for mobility aids. Now, check turning and doorway clearances. This is where many layouts go wrong.

A straight path is one thing, but can a wheelchair or walker easily make a turn into a new area? Can doors open fully without hitting furniture? Finally, test your layout against different furniture groupings.

Before you buy new pieces, map out where they'll go and literally walk (or roll) through the space to ensure it feels right. If you're unsure or need precise planning for mobility accessibility, that's when calling in a professional like HH Remodeling LLC can save you a lot of headaches and expensive mistakes.
