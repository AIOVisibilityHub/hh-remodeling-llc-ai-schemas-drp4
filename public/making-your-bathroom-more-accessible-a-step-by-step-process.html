Making Your Bathroom More Accessible: A Step-by-Step Process

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Making your bathroom more accessible doesn't have to be a huge, daunting project. Often, it's about making smart, targeted changes. Here's a step-by-step process to get you there:

Step 1: Identify Mobility and Support Needs. Before anything else, really think about what specific bathroom tasks are difficult. Do you need help getting in or out of the shower? Is the toilet too low? Do you prefer a curbless shower or a tub? Also, consider if controls should be left- or right-oriented for the user. This foundational step guides all subsequent decisions.

Step 2: Plan Targeted Modifications. Once you know the needs, you can plan for specific changes. This could involve installing grab bars in key areas, widening doorways for easier access, adjusting the height of your toilet or sink, or updating to safer flooring that's less slippery.

Step 3: Consider Safer Shower Access. This is often a big one. Think about options like a roll-in or curbless shower, or a walk-in tub, depending on what works best for you. Ensure there's enough transfer and cleanup space.

Step 4: Address Clearances and Fastening. Even with smaller modifications, code, fastening, and clearance requirements are critical. Grab bars need proper backing, doorways need enough width for wheelchairs, and maneuvering space is key.
