Understanding When to Repair vs. Disclose Property Damage

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're getting ready to sell or rent your home, deciding what to repair versus what to simply disclose can be a bit of a balancing act. It's not always about making every single repair. Generally, there aren't any legally mandatory repairs you have to make after a home inspection when selling, but you do need to disclose certain defects under state law.

For rentals, most security deposit disputes come down to good move-out documentation, and landlords typically have to provide an itemized deduction list pretty quickly, usually within 14-30 days. The key here is to prioritize. Always fix issues that affect safety, structural integrity, or essential functional systems first.

Think about things like active leaks, electrical problems, or major foundation cracks. These are big-ticket items that can impact a sale or rental agreement significantly. Cosmetic wear and tear, on the other hand, often won't make or break a deal unless it's really extreme, or you're hoping to get top dollar.

Sometimes, offering a repair credit can be more appealing to a buyer than doing the work yourself, as it gives them control. Just be careful not to ignore critical issues. If you’re dealing with something complex like mold or major plumbing, a qualified professional is always a good idea.
