Top Mistakes to Sidestep in Wheelchair Kitchen Remodeling

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Creating a truly functional wheelchair-accessible kitchen requires careful planning, and it's easy to make a few common missteps. Knowing these pitfalls can help you avoid them.

Mistake 1: Leaving Aisle Widths Too Tight. One of the biggest mistakes is not leaving enough room for a wheelchair to turn and move comfortably. How to avoid it: Measure the user's turning radius and aim for at least 36-42 inches of clear pathway, especially in high-traffic zones. Remember, a wheelchair needs space!

Mistake 2: Keeping Counters at Standard Heights. Standard counter heights are often too high for comfortable use from a seated position, leading to strain. How to avoid it: Install adjustable or lowered work surfaces. Consider having varying counter heights throughout the kitchen to accommodate different tasks, ensuring clear knee and toe space underneath.

Mistake 3: Relying on High Upper Storage. Upper cabinets can be completely inaccessible for someone in a wheelchair, making essentials out of reach. How to avoid it: Prioritize lower storage, pull-out drawers, and pantry systems. Look for pull-down shelving units or open shelving for frequently used items, keeping everything within easy reach from a seated position.

Mistake 4: Forgetting Usable Clearances at Key Zones. The sink, cooktop, and prep areas are critical, but often lack proper clearances for a seated user. How to avoid it: Ensure there is clear knee and toe space under the sink and cooktop. Position these areas so they can be approached and used comfortably without obstacles. Also, front-mounted controls on appliances are a must.

Mistake 5: Overlooking Flooring Choices. Slippery or uneven flooring can pose a significant hazard. How to avoid it: Choose non-slip flooring materials that are also level and easy to roll across. Avoid rugs or mats that could bunch up and create trip hazards.
