Why isn't my wheelchair accessible vanity working for me?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

If you’ve installed a wheelchair accessible vanity and it’s still not quite hitting the mark, you're not alone. There are a few key areas where installations often fall short, preventing true ease of use. A major issue can be the counter or rim height – if it’s above the 34-inch maximum, a wheelchair user won't be able to comfortably roll up and use the sink. You also need sufficient clear floor space, typically around 30 by 48 inches, to allow for proper approach and turning. Without this, the vanity is just a barrier.

Another critical factor is knee and toe clearance. The space under the sink should ideally be about 27 inches high, 30 inches wide, and 19 inches deep to accommodate knees and toes. Many standard vanities or even poorly designed accessible ones fail to provide this, leaving plumbing exposed and unprotected, which can be a burn hazard.

Also, if the sink bowl is too deep, it can make reaching the faucet difficult, negating the point of the accessible height. Sometimes, people assume a wall-mounted vanity automatically solves all problems, but without checking the specific dimensions and clearances, it might still not be fully functional.

Ensuring all these elements are correctly addressed is vital for a truly functional wheelchair accessible vanity.
