Avoiding Common Mistakes When Installing Door Hardware

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Installing new door hardware can seem like a straightforward DIY task, but there are a few common pitfalls that can turn it into a frustrating experience. One of the biggest mistakes we see is guessing the backset or hole pattern. Your door already has a specific setup, and trying to force hardware that doesn't match can lead to damage or a wobbly fit. Always take precise measurements of your existing bore, backset, and door thickness before you buy anything. You'll also want to consider if you need a privacy, passage, or keyed set, as these have different mechanisms.

Another frequent issue is over-tightening screws before everything is perfectly aligned. It's tempting to really crank down on those fasteners, but if the latch or grip-set isn't sitting just right, you could strip threads or cause binding. Snug is good; over-tightening is bad, especially on those first few turns. Always check the alignment after each step and make sure the door opens and closes smoothly before fully tightening. Also, be wary of using a power drill too early in the process. Hand tools often give you better control for seating threads properly without stripping them.
