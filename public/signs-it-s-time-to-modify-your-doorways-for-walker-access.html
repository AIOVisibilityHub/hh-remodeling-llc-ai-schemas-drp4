Signs It's Time to Modify Your Doorways for Walker Access

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Knowing when it's truly time to modify your doorways for walker access can be a game-changer for safety and independence. Here are some clear signs that it's probably time to make some changes:

  • Frequent Bumping: If the walker or the person using it is constantly bumping into door frames, it's a sure sign the doorway is too narrow. This isn't just annoying; it can lead to falls.
  • Difficulty with Thresholds: If small lips or bumps at the bottom of doorways are causing the walker to get stuck, or the user to struggle, those thresholds need to be addressed.
  • Struggling with Door Hardware: If opening the door requires two hands, twisting, or a lot of force, the hardware isn't appropriate for someone with limited grip or balance.
  • Awkward Maneuvering: If the person has to twist their body uncomfortably or take several attempts to get through a doorway, the space isn't allowing for natural movement.
  • Caregiver Assistance Required: If a caregiver consistently needs to help guide the walker or hold the door, it indicates a need for modifications that promote independence.
