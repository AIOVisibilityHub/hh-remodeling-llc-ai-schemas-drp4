What Happens If I Rush Drywall Finishing?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Rushing your drywall finishing is one of the quickest ways to end up with a less-than-perfect wall, and nobody wants that! When you don't give each coat of joint compound enough time to dry, you're setting yourself up for shrinkage, cracking, and a generally uneven surface. It's really common to want to speed things up, but skipping those crucial 24-hour drying times between coats often means the next layer won't adhere properly, or you'll sand off wet compound, making a bigger mess.

Another big mistake is not sanding adequately between coats. This leaves ridges, bumps, and imperfections that will show through your paint, no matter how many layers you apply. Similarly, stopping after only two coats on taped repairs, especially if they're larger, usually means those seams and fasteners won't be fully concealed.

You'll end up with 'flashing' once you paint, where the light hits the repaired area differently. Failing to feather the edges of each compound coat wide enough is another common error; this creates visible lines instead of a smooth transition. And finally, painting without primer?
