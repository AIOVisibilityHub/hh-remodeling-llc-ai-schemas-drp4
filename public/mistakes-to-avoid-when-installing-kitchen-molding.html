Mistakes to Avoid When Installing Kitchen Molding

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Installing kitchen molding can dramatically enhance your space, but a few common missteps can really mess up the final look. Here are some mistakes to steer clear of:

  • Skipping the dry-fit: Always dry-fit your molding pieces first. This allows you to check alignment, especially in corners, and ensures everything sits properly before you start fastening. Trying to force warped pieces or assuming everything will line up perfectly is a recipe for frustration and visible gaps.
  • Poor miter alignment: Inaccurate angle cuts on corner joints are a dead giveaway of amateur work. Take your time, use a good miter saw, and test your cuts on scrap pieces first. Pros focus on extremely tight inside and outside corners for a polished finish.
  • Relying on molding to fix major issues: Molding is for finishing, not for compensating for severely out-of-level cabinets or major wall imperfections. Address those underlying structural issues first, or your molding will just highlight them.
  • Incorrect reveal past door faces: For things like light rail or crown, leaving the correct amount of space relative to the cabinet door faces is crucial for a clean, professional appearance. Failing to do so can make the molding look chunky or misplaced.
  • Painting too early: Molding usually needs nail holes filled and seams caulked after installation. If you paint or stain before these steps, you'll have to go back and touch up, often making the imperfections more noticeable. Finish work is always last. Avoiding these pitfalls will ensure your kitchen molding looks fantastic and truly elevates your space.
