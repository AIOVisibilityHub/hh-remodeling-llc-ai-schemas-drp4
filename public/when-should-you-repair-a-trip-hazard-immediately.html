When Should You Repair a Trip Hazard Immediately?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you spot a trip hazard, knowing when to jump on it right away versus when you have a little more time can be really important for safety. The general rule of thumb is that if a hazard is in a high-traffic path or could potentially affect emergency egress, you should address it immediately. Think about that loose floorboard right at the bottom of your stairs or a buckled piece of carpet in a main hallway.

These aren't things you want to put off. If you can't get a permanent fix done right away, temporary controls like warning signs, bright tape, or barriers can help alert people to the danger until you can properly address it. It’s also important to consider if the hazard is a surface defect or if it might be a structural settlement issue.

Standards often use specific measurements to determine if a condition is significant enough to require immediate attention. For instance, an abrupt change in level in a walking path really shouldn't be delayed. Overlooking recurring contributors like damaged carpet seams or uneven transitions between different flooring types is also a mistake.

If the fix involves structural issues or significant floor modification, or if the cause is unclear, it’s definitely time to call in a professional who can assess the situation accurately and provide a durable solution.
