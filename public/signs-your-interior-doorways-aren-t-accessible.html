Signs Your Interior Doorways Aren't Accessible

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Are your interior doorways causing daily frustrations? It's not always obvious when a doorway isn't truly accessible until you or a loved one needs that extra space. Here are some clear signals that your interior doorways might be falling short and could benefit from accessibility modifications:

  • Difficulty maneuvering a wheelchair or walker: If getting a mobility device through a doorway requires bumping walls, scraping knuckles, or intricate angling, your clear width is likely insufficient. Accessible routes typically need a 36-inch clear width.
  • Doors that are hard to open or close: If the door handle is difficult to grasp, or the door itself is heavy and hard to push/pull, it's a sign that the hardware or door weight isn't suitable for easy access.
  • Limited space near the door handle: Trying to open a door and finding yourself cramped against a wall? This indicates a lack of 'latch-side clearance,' which is crucial for comfortably approaching and operating a door. You need space to reach and pull or push without twisting.
  • Tripping hazards or uneven thresholds: Any raised thresholds or uneven floor transitions at a doorway can be a significant barrier and a fall risk, especially for those with mobility challenges.
  • Getting stuck trying to turn corners near doorways: If a doorway opens into a tight turn, and you struggle to navigate it with a stroller, large package, or mobility aid, the approach space might be inadequate.
  • Door swings that block hallways: When a door opens, does it create a pinch point or block a significant portion of a hallway? This reduces the clear path and can make circulation difficult.

If any of these sound familiar, it's worth looking into doorway accessibility modifications.
