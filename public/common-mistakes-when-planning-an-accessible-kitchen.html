Common Mistakes When Planning an Accessible Kitchen

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're planning an accessible kitchen, it's easy to overlook some details that can make a big difference. One common mistake is ignoring knee clearance under worktops and sinks. It's not enough for a counter to be low; you also need proper space underneath for seated access.

Another frequent oversight is placing storage too high, making it difficult to reach essential items. Similarly, leaving narrow passages can create frustrating bottlenecks, especially for those using mobility aids. A big error we often see is treating the counter, sink, and appliance areas as isolated elements instead of a single, integrated workflow.

This can lead to awkward transitions and inefficient use of space. Forgetting to account for specific user mobility needs early in the planning process is another pitfall. Getting these elements right from the start is critical.

If your project involves custom clearances, plumbing, electrical changes, or appliance integration, bringing in a professional can prevent costly reworks and ensure everything meets your specific requirements.
