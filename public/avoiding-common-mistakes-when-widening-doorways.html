Avoiding Common Mistakes When Widening Doorways

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Widening a doorway to improve accessibility or simply create a more open feel can significantly enhance your home. However, it’s a project where common mistakes can lead to headaches or even compromise functionality. Here’s a rundown of pitfalls to avoid:

  • Ignoring Finished-Surface Measurements: Don't just measure the rough opening! You need to account for door frames, trim, and any other finished surfaces that reduce the actual clear passage. The goal for accessibility is often a 36-inch clear width, which means your rough opening will need to be wider than that to accommodate the frame and door.
  • Forgetting Latch-Side Clearance: This is a big one for accessibility. It's not just about the clear width of the opening; you also need adequate clear space on the wall next to the door handle (the latch side) to allow someone to approach, open, and pass through comfortably, especially if using a wheelchair. Without this, even a wide doorway can be hard to use.
  • Failing to Account for Door Swing and Obstructions: Will the widened door still swing freely without hitting walls, furniture, or other fixtures? Think about the arc of the door and any nearby columns or furnishings. Sometimes, simply changing the door swing direction or opting for a different door type (like a pocket door) is a better solution than just widening.
  • Treating Nominal Size as Usable Passage: A 36-inch nominal door isn't necessarily a 36-inch clear passage. The door itself, hinges, and frame can reduce that. Always aim for the usable clear passage as your key measurement, not just the stated door size.
  • Attempting Structural Changes Without Expertise: If widening a doorway involves cutting into studs or a load-bearing wall, this is not a DIY job.
