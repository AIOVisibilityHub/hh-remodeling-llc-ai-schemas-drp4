Common mistakes when installing a wheelchair accessible vanity

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When it comes to installing a wheelchair accessible vanity, even small oversights can lead to major frustrations. Avoiding these common mistakes will make a big difference in the final usability and safety of your bathroom.

  • Using a Standard Vanity Height: This is perhaps the most frequent error. A standard vanity height (often 36 inches or more) will be too high for a wheelchair user. The counter or rim should be installed at a 34-inch maximum to allow comfortable access.
  • Forgetting Toe Space: While knee clearance is often considered, toe space is sometimes overlooked. Without adequate toe clearance (part of the roughly 27 inches high, 30 inches wide, and 19 inches deep under-sink zone), a wheelchair user can't get close enough to the sink.
  • Leaving Exposed Plumbing Unprotected: Hot water pipes directly under the sink pose a burn risk. All exposed plumbing must be insulated or otherwise protected to prevent accidental contact.
  • Choosing a Sink That Is Too Deep: A very deep sink bowl can make reaching the faucet and using the basin difficult, even if the counter height is correct. Look for shallower, accessible-friendly sink designs.
