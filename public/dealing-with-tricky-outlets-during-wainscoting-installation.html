Dealing with Tricky Outlets During Wainscoting Installation

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Installing wainscoting can instantly upgrade a room, but those pesky electrical outlets can throw a wrench in your plans. Dealing with outlets requires careful planning and precise cuts to ensure both a beautiful finish and electrical safety. The most common questions we hear are about how to work around them without ruining the aesthetic.

First, when you're planning your layout, try to adjust your panel spacing slightly to avoid having an outlet fall right in the middle of a stile or an intricate design element. Sometimes this isn't possible, so you'll need to measure the outlet box precisely. You'll cut an opening in your wainscoting panel or rail that's just big enough for the electrical box to fit through, ensuring there's enough space for the outlet cover plate to sit flush.

Remember, it's safer to make the cut a little small and then enlarge it, rather than cutting too big initially. Always make sure the power is off to that outlet before you start cutting or working near it! You might also need to extend the outlet box forward with box extenders so that the receptacle is flush with the new wainscoting surface.

This is a critical safety step to prevent fire hazards.
