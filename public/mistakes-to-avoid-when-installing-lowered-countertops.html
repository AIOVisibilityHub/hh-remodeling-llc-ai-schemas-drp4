Mistakes to Avoid When Installing Lowered Countertops

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Installing a lowered countertop can be a game-changer for kitchen accessibility, but it's easy to make some common mistakes that can hinder its effectiveness. One frequent error is using a height that is low enough for seated access but failing to provide enough knee space underneath. A countertop needs to be deep enough for knees and toes to comfortably fit.

Another mistake is not leaving an adequate adjacent landing area. You'll want space next to a lowered sink or cooktop for placing items, just as you would with a standard counter. Lowering the counter without adjusting storage and sink height to match the user’s reach is another pitfall; this can defeat the purpose of the modification.

Also, neglecting to consider how the new height affects the backsplash, edging, or adjoining worktop runs can lead to a disjointed look or functional issues. Finally, sometimes people focus solely on the counter and forget to ensure reachable controls for any appliances integrated into that section. For installations that involve structural support, plumbing, or electrical work, or if you're aiming to meet specific accessibility standards, it's wise to consult with a professional.
