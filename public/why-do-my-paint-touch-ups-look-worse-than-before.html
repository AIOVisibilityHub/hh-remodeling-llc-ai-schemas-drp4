Why Do My Paint Touch-Ups Look Worse Than Before?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

It's super frustrating, isn't it? You try to fix a little scuff on the wall, and now you've got a shiny spot or a noticeable patch that stands out more than the original mark. Often, this happens because the touch-up paint isn't blending with the existing finish.

Maybe you're using an old can of paint that's changed color over time, or the sheen has developed differently on the wall compared to the fresh paint. Sometimes, people use a brush on a wall that was rolled, which creates a different texture. It could also be that you're painting over dirt or loose bits, or you're using too much paint, creating a thick layer that just can't quite disappear.

When you try to touch up, it's really easy to create what's called a 'sheen halo' if you're not careful with how you apply the paint, especially on smooth walls. If you're finding your touch-ups are making things worse, it might be time to consider if the problem is beyond a simple spot fix. If you've tried a few times and it's still not looking right, or if the area you're trying to fix is larger than just a few tiny spots, a more extensive repaint might be needed to get that consistent look you're after.

That's when getting some professional guidance can really help achieve that seamless finish.
