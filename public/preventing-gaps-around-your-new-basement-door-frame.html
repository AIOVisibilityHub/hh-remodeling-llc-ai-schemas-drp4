Preventing Gaps Around Your New Basement Door Frame

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

It's super frustrating to install a new door frame in your basement only to find unsightly gaps appear around the edges. But with a little attention to detail, you can totally avoid this common headache. One of the biggest mistakes people make is not properly checking for level and plumb before fastening anything permanently.

Basements often have uneven floors and walls, so assuming your rough opening is perfect is a recipe for gaps. Always dry-fit your frame and use shims to get everything perfectly square and plumb before you nail it in. If you force a frame into an irregular opening, it’s going to show, usually at the trim lines.

Another critical point is the trim itself. Just like with molding, careful measuring and cutting, especially for mitered corners, are essential. If your trim pieces are cut too short or not aligned correctly, you'll end up with visible gaps.

Use caulk and wood filler generously to seal any small gaps and cover nail holes for a really smooth, professional finish.
