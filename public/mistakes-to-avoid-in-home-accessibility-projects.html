Mistakes to Avoid in Home Accessibility Projects

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're making your home more accessible, it's easy to overlook crucial details that can make a big difference. Avoiding these common mistakes can save you time, money, and frustration.

  • Skipping a full-home assessment: Don't just focus on one room. A complete room-by-room survey helps you identify all potential barriers, not just the obvious ones. You might miss critical issues in less-used areas that still impact overall mobility.
  • Underestimating turning and clear floor space: Wheelchairs and other mobility devices need ample room to maneuver. Failing to account for sufficient turning space and clear floor areas can leave you with modifications that are still difficult to use.
  • Choosing hard-to-operate hardware or fixtures: While aesthetics are important, functionality comes first. Opting for doorknobs, faucets, or switches that are still challenging to grip or turn defeats the purpose of an accessible remodel.
  • Not planning for future needs: Accessibility isn't just for today. Think about how your mobility might change over time and incorporate features that will continue to serve you in the long run.
  • Ignoring professional help for complex changes: If your project involves structural modifications, major plumbing, or electrical work, or if it impacts ramps or stairs, a qualified professional is essential. They ensure everything is safe, up to code, and truly functional.

By keeping these points in mind, you can create an accessible home that genuinely enhances your independence and comfort.
