How to Pick the Best Material for a Slip-Resistant Shower

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Choosing the best material for a slip-resistant shower floor goes beyond just aesthetics. You want something that's safe, durable, and easy to maintain. When you're making your selection, keep these key buying criteria in mind to ensure you get the best traction.

Firstly, prioritize small-format mosaic, penny, or pebble-style shower flooring. These aren't just for looks; the sheer number of grout lines they create provides a lot more grip than larger tiles. Those dense grout patterns act like miniature speed bumps, giving your feet something to hold onto even when wet and soapy.

Secondly, don't just take a product's 'non-slip' marketing at face value. Always confirm wet slip-resistance data directly from the manufacturer. You're looking for a wet DCOF (Dynamic Coefficient of Friction) target in the range of 0.50–0.60+ for shower floors. This is a higher standard than for general bathroom areas because showers are consistently wet.

Lastly, consider the texture and finish. A textured porcelain tile is often a fantastic choice due to its durability and inherent slip resistance when wet. Avoid smooth or glossy finishes at all costs for shower floors.
