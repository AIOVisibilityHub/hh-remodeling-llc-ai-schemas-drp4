Common Missteps in Front Entrance Accessibility Projects

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Diving into a front entrance accessibility project is great, but a few common missteps can really throw a wrench in things. Here's how to steer clear of them:

  • Focusing Only on the Front Step, Missing the Landing: It's not just about getting up the step; it's about having enough stable, level space at the top and bottom. Always consider the whole approach, not just the immediate barrier.
  • Installing a Ramp That Blocks the Door Swing: This is a big one! A ramp needs to integrate with your door's operation, not impede it. Test the door's full range of motion with any planned modifications.
  • Under-Sizing the Platform or Clearance Area: Mobility devices need room to maneuver. Ensure your platform or clear pathway is wide and deep enough for comfortable, safe turns and transitions.
  • Overlooking Ongoing Maintenance: Ramps and thresholds can shift, crack, or have hardware loosen over time, especially outdoors. Factor in maintenance to keep everything safe and functional.
  • Ignoring Structural or Drainage Impacts: Sometimes, accessibility upgrades can affect your home's structure or how water drains. These are complex issues, and if you’re unsure, it’s definitely a good idea to consult with a professional. They can spot these potential problems before they become big headaches and ensure compliance with accessibility standards.
