What to Do When Your Old Rug Won't Budge

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

You've decided to pull up that old rug, but it's proving to be a real challenge. Don't worry, you're not alone! Many older installations can be stubborn, especially if they've been down for years.

The first step is usually to try and free up a corner. Sometimes you can get leverage with a pair of pliers or a strong pry bar. Once you have a bit of it loose, try to pull it back at a low angle.

If the rug is large or heavily glued, cutting it into manageable strips can make a huge difference, making it much safer and faster than trying to tackle the whole room at once. After the rug is up, you'll likely deal with the pad, tack strips, and adhesive. Make sure you have a plan for sweeping and vacuuming the subfloor, and then meticulously removing any remaining staples or adhesive.

Adhesive-backed carpet, in particular, might need extra scraping or even some gentle warming to release its grip. If you're finding yourself wrestling with a massive, glued-down carpet or worried about damaging your subfloor, it might be time to bring in the pros.
