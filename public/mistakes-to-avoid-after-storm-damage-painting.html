Mistakes to Avoid After Storm Damage Painting

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

It's easy to want your home to look normal again after a storm, but rushing the painting process can lead to costly mistakes. Here are some common pitfalls to avoid when you're dealing with storm-related painting and finish work, along with tips on how to prevent them:

  • Painting Over Damp Surfaces: This is a huge no-no. If the underlying materials, like drywall or wood, are still damp, paint won't adhere properly and will likely peel, blister, or even foster mold growth down the line. How to avoid it: Always ensure affected areas are thoroughly dried, ideally with professional moisture testing, before any paint is applied.
  • Failing to Identify Hidden Damage: What you see on the surface might not be the full extent of the damage. Water can travel and compromise areas behind walls or ceilings. How to avoid it: Conduct a meticulous damage assessment, and consider a professional inspection to uncover any unseen issues before you start painting.
  • Skipping Crucial Prep Work: Storm-damaged surfaces often require more than just a quick wipe-down. This could mean extensive cleaning, sanding, filling, and even replacing sections of material. How to avoid it: Never underestimate the amount of preparation needed. Ensure all debris is removed, surfaces are stable, and any necessary repairs are made before priming or painting.
  • Using the Wrong Primer/Sealer: Damaged areas, especially those with water stains, may require specific primers or sealers to prevent stains from bleeding through the new paint. How to avoid it: Consult with an expert to select the correct primers and sealers for the specific type of storm damage your home has sustained.
  • Underestimating the Project Scope: Storm damage often involves more than just painting; it might include drywall repair, carpentry, or even mold remediation. How to avoid it: Get a comprehensive estimate that details all necessary repairs and finish work, not just the painting itself.
