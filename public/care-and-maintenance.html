When modifying a bathroom for accessibility, it’s easy to make choices that seem helpful but actually create new problems or simply don't work for the user. One of the biggest mistakes is installing grab bars or other accessories without checking for proper wall backing. You need solid support behind those fixtures, otherwise, they can pull right out of the wall when weight is applied, leading to falls.

Another common misstep is choosing a layout that blocks maneuvering space. What looks good on paper might not allow enough room for a wheelchair or walker to turn, or for a caregiver to assist. Always consider the actual circulation path!

Using slippery surfaces is also a frequent mistake. While certain tiles might look great, if they become slick when wet, they're a hazard for anyone with limited mobility. Opt for slip-resistant flooring.

Lastly, don't make changes that look accessible but don’t fit the user’s actual transfer and reach needs. Every person's requirements are unique, so what works for one might not work for another. It's really worth getting professional advice to ensure the modifications truly enhance usability and safety.

It's not always obvious whether your deck just needs a few new boards or if you're looking at a more extensive repair. The key is to assess the extent of the damage. Start by walking around your deck and really paying attention to what you feel and see. Are there just a few isolated soft spots, or does a large section feel weak? Look for specific issues like rot, noticeable cracks, splintering, or boards that are clearly warped or bowing. Raised fasteners can also be a clue that the wood is deteriorating around them.

If the issues are contained to just a handful of surface boards, and the underlying joists and substructure feel solid and look free of rot, then a simple deck board replacement or “resurfacing” might be all you need. This involves carefully removing the damaged boards, preparing the joists, and installing new, matching decking. However, if you find that the rot has spread to the joists, beams, or other critical structural components, or if there's significant movement in multiple areas, you might be facing a larger repair that goes beyond just the surface.

Signs Your Deck Railing Needs Professional Repair

Read article

While some minor railing issues can be a DIY fix, there are clear indicators that it's time to call in a professional for your deck railing repair. Your deck railing is a critical safety component, especially on elevated decks, so ignoring these signs can lead to dangerous situations.

  • Posts are loose at the deck connection: This is perhaps the most serious sign. If the main support posts for your railing are wobbly where they meet the deck surface or rim joist, the entire railing system is compromised. This often requires structural reinforcement.
  • Visible wood rot or metal corrosion at attachment points: Inspect where the railing attaches to the deck, particularly at the base of posts. If you see significant wood rot or rust/corrosion on metal fasteners, it means the integrity of the connection is failing, even if the railing still looks okay from a distance.
  • The railing fails the 'push test' significantly: Give your railing a firm push. If it moves more than a small amount or feels unstable, it’s not safe. This is especially true if the movement is from the base of the posts.
  • Cracked or broken structural components: If you notice cracks in the posts, main rails, or other load-bearing parts of the railing, it's a red flag. These can compromise the railing's ability to withstand pressure.
  • Railing on an elevated deck: For any deck that's off the ground, a failing railing presents a severe fall hazard. Don't take chances with elevated deck railings; always opt for professional assessment if you have concerns.

When these issues arise, particularly involving the structural integrity of your railing, getting professional help from an experienced contractor like Frasheski Construction ensures your deck is secure and up to safety standards.

How to Stabilize a Wobbly Fence Post

How to Stabilize a Wobbly Fence Post

Read article

Dealing with a wobbly fence post? It's a common issue in the East Bay, but often fixable if caught early! Here's a quick guide on what to do.

Step 1: Assess the Damage and Call 811

First, figure out what's making it wobbly. Is the post rotten at the base? Is the concrete footing loose? Or has the soil around it just shifted? Before you start digging, though, remember to call 811! This is super important to get any underground utilities marked so you don't accidentally hit a pipe or cable.

Step 2: Expose the Post Base

Carefully dig around the base of the wobbly post. You'll want to expose the entire footing. This will help you see if the concrete is cracked, if the post itself is rotted below ground, or if it's just loose in its setting.

Step 3: Repair or Replace

If the post itself is still sound but just loose in its footing, you might be able to re-set it. Brace the post so it's perfectly plumb (straight up and down), then pour new concrete around the base. If the post is rotten or badly damaged, you're better off replacing it entirely. Dig out the old post and footing, set a new post, brace it, and pour fresh concrete. Make sure to let the concrete cure fully before putting any stress on the fence.
