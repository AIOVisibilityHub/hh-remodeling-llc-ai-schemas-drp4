How to ensure your wheelchair accessible vanity meets all needs

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Getting a wheelchair accessible vanity right involves a few key steps to make sure it's truly functional and safe. Here's how to approach it:

Step 1: Confirm Clear Floor Space. Before anything else, ensure there's enough room for a wheelchair to approach and turn. This usually means a clear floor space of about 30 by 48 inches in front of the vanity. Without this, even the best vanity won't be truly accessible.

Step 2: Set the Correct Counter Height. The counter or sink rim needs to be installed at an accessible height, typically a 34-inch maximum. This allows a wheelchair user to comfortably reach the basin and controls. Precision here is crucial, so measure twice!

Step 3: Provide Ample Knee and Toe Clearance. This is where many installations falter. The space under the sink should be approximately 27 inches high, 30 inches wide, and 19 inches deep to accommodate knees and toes. Make sure there are no cabinet parts or obstructions in this zone.

Step 4: Protect Exposed Plumbing. If any plumbing is exposed under the sink, it's essential to insulate or otherwise protect it to prevent burns. This is a common oversight that can lead to safety issues.

Step 5: Test Approach and Usability. After installation, have a wheelchair user test the vanity. Can they roll up close? Is the faucet easy to reach and operate? Does anything feel awkward or difficult? This real-world test is invaluable.
