How to Safely Remove Kitchen Cabinets Without Damage

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Removing kitchen cabinets can be a dusty and sometimes challenging job, but with the right approach, you can do it safely and minimize damage to your walls and floors. Here’s a step-by-step guide to help you through the process:

Step 1: Empty and Protect. First, completely empty all contents from the cabinets. This is also a good time to clear the surrounding area of any appliances, furniture, or fragile items. Lay down drop cloths or old blankets to protect your floors from falling debris and potential damage.

Step 2: Disconnect Utilities (If Applicable). If any cabinets house a sink, dishwasher, or have electrical outlets, ensure all water and power supplies are safely disconnected at the main shut-off and breaker box. It’s critical to double-check that no live wires or active water lines are present before proceeding.

Step 3: Remove Doors and Drawers. Start by taking off all cabinet doors and then removing the drawers. This significantly lightens the load and makes the larger cabinet boxes much easier to handle. Store the hardware (hinges, pulls) if you plan to reuse or donate the cabinets.

Step 4: Detach Upper Cabinets. Upper cabinets should be removed before base cabinets. Working with a helper, locate the screws securing the cabinets to the wall studs – usually found inside the cabinet boxes. Support the cabinet from below, unscrew it from the wall, and carefully lower it. Be mindful of screws hidden behind corner fillers or trim pieces. These can often be overlooked.

Step 5: Detach Base Cabinets. Once the uppers are down, move to the base cabinets. Again, locate all screws securing them to the wall studs and to each other. You may also find screws anchoring them to the floor. Carefully detach these and lift the cabinets away. Watch out for any remaining glue or caulk that might be bonding them to the wall or floor.

Step 6: Repair and Clean Up. After all cabinets are removed, you'll likely have some drywall damage, exposed utility rough-ins, or areas needing patching.
