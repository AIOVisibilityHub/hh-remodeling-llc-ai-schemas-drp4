How to tell if your log cabin trim needs patching or replacing

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're looking at damaged trim on your log cabin, it can be tricky to know if you can just patch it up or if it needs a full replacement. You'll want to inspect the trim for a few things: is the wood soft or spongy? Are there any splits, open joints, or failing fasteners?

And is there any evidence of water getting in? If you see active decay, especially if it's deep, a simple patch won't cut it. You've got to remove that deteriorated material first.

Cosmetic patching without addressing the moisture source is a common mistake that just leads to more problems down the road. Also, think about how well a patch will match the existing log or cabin profile and finish. If the damage is extensive, or if the trim is structural or highly visible, replacing it often gives you a much better and longer-lasting result.

If you're dealing with rot that extends into the adjoining logs or if you need help matching complex profiles, that's definitely a time when a qualified professional can make a huge difference. We can help you decide on the best course of action.
