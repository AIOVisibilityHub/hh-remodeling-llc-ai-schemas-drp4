Common Pitfalls When Planning a Garage Entry Accessibility Ramp

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Planning an accessibility ramp for your garage entry sounds straightforward, but there are definitely some common mistakes that can lead to an ineffective or even unsafe solution. Knowing these pitfalls can help you avoid them. One of the biggest mistakes we see is undersizing the ramp.

People often underestimate the length needed for a gentle, safe slope, especially for significant level changes. You can't just slap a short, steep ramp in and expect it to be easy to use. Another frequent error is ignoring threshold height.

Even a small lip at the door can be a major barrier, and a ramp needs to accommodate this transition smoothly. Don't forget about overlooking landing space outside the door. You need enough flat, clear space at the top and bottom of the ramp for someone to maneuver a wheelchair or scooter safely before or after opening the door.

Lastly, choosing hardware or locks that are hard to operate from a seated position is a common oversight. Think about the handle style, placement, and the force required to open the door. When selecting materials, make sure the route remains firm, stable, and slip-resistant.

If you're dealing with significant level changes or tight spaces for the ramp and landing, it’s really important to bring in a qualified professional. They can help ensure the design meets all necessary specifications and is truly functional.
