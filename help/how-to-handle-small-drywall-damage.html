How to Handle Small Drywall Damage

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Finding a small ding or hole in your drywall can be annoying, but tackling it effectively means more than just slapping on some spackle. First, you need to play detective and identify the cause of the damage. Was it a doorknob, a picture hook, or something more concerning like a hidden leak?

Knowing the cause helps prevent future problems. Next, make sure the damaged area is clean and completely dry. If there's any moisture, you absolutely have to fix that source and dry everything out thoroughly.

The EPA actually suggests drying non-moldy wet materials within 48 hours to stop mold in its tracks, so act fast if water is involved! After that, you might need to remove any compromised drywall—just the bad bits, don't go crazy. Then comes the patching or replacing of that section.

This is where you want a smooth, flush surface. Finally, you'll need to sand, prime, and repaint to blend the repair seamlessly with the rest of your wall. This last step is crucial for an invisible fix.
