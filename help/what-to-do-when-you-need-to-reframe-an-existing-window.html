What to Do When You Need to Reframe an Existing Window

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're looking to change an existing window opening, whether to enlarge it or simply reconfigure it, there are some important steps to take to ensure the job is done safely and correctly. First, and this is super crucial, turn off the power to the wall you'll be working on. You don't want any surprises from hidden electrical lines!

Next, you'll need to carefully locate any plumbing or other utilities that might be running through that section of the wall. Sometimes, you'll have to remove drywall and insulation to get a clear picture of what's inside. Once you know what you're dealing with, you can carefully cut out any existing studs where your new or reconfigured opening will go.

It's vital to do this precisely so you don't damage surrounding framing that you want to keep. After clearing the space, you’ll then rebuild the opening with proper framing members, just like you would for a new window. This includes new king studs, jack studs, a header, and a sill with cripples.

This ensures the opening is structurally sound and ready for your new window. Remember, remodeling existing walls often hides surprises that new construction doesn't, so experience with remodel sequencing and utility coordination is key. If you're tackling a load-bearing wall, dealing with concealed utilities, or trying to correct an opening that's already out of spec, that's when a qualified professional becomes essential.
