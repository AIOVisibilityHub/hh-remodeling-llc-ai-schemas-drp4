Tackling Uneven Walls: A Guide to Crown Molding Installation

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

So, you're ready to install crown molding, but your walls and ceilings aren't perfectly straight? Welcome to the club! This is super common, especially in older homes, but it doesn't mean you can't have beautiful crown molding.

The secret lies in careful planning and execution. First, measure your room meticulously and mark your stud locations. When it comes to cutting, accurate miters and cope joints are critical, but even more so with uneven surfaces.

You'll often need to do some test cuts and dry-fitting to see how the molding sits. For those tricky angles, you might need to scribe the molding to fit the contours of your walls or ceiling, which basically means custom-fitting it. After fastening, filling any gaps with caulk and nail holes with putty is crucial for a seamless look.

Remember, the goal is to make it look like everything is perfectly straight, even if it's not. If you've got significant irregularities or very ornate molding, that's when a professional trim carpenter can really shine. They have the experience and tools to make those imperfections disappear, ensuring a high-quality finish that looks fantastic.
