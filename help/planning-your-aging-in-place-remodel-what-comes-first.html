Planning Your Aging-in-Place Remodel: What Comes First?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Getting started on an aging-in-place remodel can feel a bit overwhelming, but breaking it down makes it much easier. The first and most crucial step is usually a solid safety assessment. Think of it as your blueprint.

This assessment helps pinpoint exactly what upgrades matter most now versus what can wait, and it ensures your changes will actually improve safety without making your home feel institutional. After the assessment, you'll want to prioritize your modifications. High-use areas like bathrooms, entrances, stairs, and bedrooms are typically tackled first because they present the most immediate safety concerns.

Think about things like better lighting, clearer pathways, and maybe some grab bars right away. It's definitely best to plan these projects before any major falls or significant mobility decline, so you're ahead of the curve. Getting a contractor who understands aging-in-place is also key; they can help coordinate all the safety-focused details like door widths, floor transitions, and proper rail placement.
