Common Pitfalls in Accessible Furniture Arrangement

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Creating an accessible living room is all about thoughtful planning, but it's easy to stumble into some common pitfalls that can undermine your efforts. As an Aurora-based remodeling company, HH Remodeling LLC helps homeowners across the Denver area create spaces that truly work for everyone. Let's look at some frequent mistakes we see in accessible furniture arrangement so you can avoid them.

One major mistake is using living-room dimensions that work visually but not functionally. A room might look balanced with furniture placed a certain way, but if it doesn't allow for comfortable movement, especially for someone with a mobility aid, it's not truly accessible. Always prioritize the functional movement path.

Another common error is underestimating door swing space. You might have a clear path leading to a door, but if a piece of furniture blocks the door from opening fully, or creates a tight pinch point when it does, you've got a problem. Always account for the full swing of any door.

A third pitfall is forgetting that a path that works for one person may fail for two people passing or for mobility-device use. A 30-inch path might be okay for one person walking, but it's not enough for two people to pass comfortably, and it's definitely not enough for a wheelchair or walker. Aim for at least 36 inches for main accessible paths, and more for turns.

Lastly, not considering the real footprint size of furniture. A sofa might look compact online, but its actual dimensions, combined with legroom and armrests, can eat up valuable circulation space. Always check precise measurements.
