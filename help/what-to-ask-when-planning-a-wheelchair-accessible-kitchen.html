What to Ask When Planning a Wheelchair Accessible Kitchen?

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Planning a wheelchair accessible kitchen means thinking about how to make every part of the space usable from a seated position. It’s a bit different than standard remodels, so asking the right questions upfront is key. You'll want to start by considering how the user navigates the space.

What’s their turning radius? This will dictate how wide your walkways need to be. Think about counter heights, too – what height works best for meal prep and other tasks when seated?

It’s often not a one-size-fits-all answer, so adjustable or split-height counters can be a real game-changer. Also, how can the sink, stove, and refrigerator be kept reachable without obstacles? You'll need clear knee and toe space under sinks and cooktops.

Don't forget storage; upper cabinets can be tough, so focus on reachable pull-out drawers and lower shelving. Every element, from appliances to controls, should be easily operated from a seated position. If you're looking to make structural changes or need specialized knowledge in accessible design, bringing in a qualified professional is always a smart choice to ensure safety and functionality.
