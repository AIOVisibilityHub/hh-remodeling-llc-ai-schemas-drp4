Key Features for an Accessible Kitchen Remodel

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

An accessible kitchen remodel isn't just about making a space compliant; it's about crafting a kitchen that's truly usable and enjoyable for individuals with varying mobility needs. When planning, several key features stand out as essential. First, think about clear floor space and turning radius.

You'll want at least a 60-inch diameter clear floor space to allow for a full 360-degree turn for a wheelchair or walker. Next, consider counter heights. Adjustable or varied counter heights are ideal, with lower sections often set around 30-34 inches for seated use.

Knee and toe clearance under sinks and work areas is non-negotiable; this allows someone in a wheelchair to get close enough to use the space comfortably. Pull-out shelving, drawer inserts, and pantry systems make items much more accessible without deep reaching or bending. Appliances should also be chosen with accessibility in mind, such as side-opening ovens, front-controlled cooktops, and refrigerators with easy-to-reach dispensers.

Finally, lever-style faucets and cabinet hardware are far easier to operate than twist knobs, especially for those with limited dexterity. This type of project often requires careful coordination of demolition, cabinetry, plumbing, and electrical work.
