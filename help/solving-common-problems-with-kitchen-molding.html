Solving Common Problems with Kitchen Molding

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Kitchen molding installation can sometimes present a few challenges, but don't worry, most common problems have straightforward solutions. One frequent headache is getting those mitered corner joints to align perfectly. If you're seeing small gaps, it often means your cuts weren't quite accurate or your walls aren't perfectly square.

Take your time with measuring and cutting, and always dry-fit before permanently attaching. For small gaps, a bit of wood filler and careful sanding before painting can work wonders. Another issue arises when molding doesn't sit flush against the cabinet or wall, creating a visible gap.

This could be due to warped molding, uneven surfaces, or not using enough attachment points. If a piece is warped, sometimes clamping it firmly while the adhesive sets can help, or you might need to replace it. For uneven walls, professional installers often use scribing techniques to custom-fit the molding, which is tough to do yourself.

Finally, getting the 'reveal' right – how far the molding extends past the cabinet door face – is crucial for a clean look. Make sure to check manufacturer guidelines or common industry practices.
