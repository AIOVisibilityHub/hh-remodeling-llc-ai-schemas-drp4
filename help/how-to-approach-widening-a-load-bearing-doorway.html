How to Approach Widening a Load-Bearing Doorway

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Widening a doorway, especially in a load-bearing wall, can feel like a daunting task, but with the right approach, it's a completely achievable home improvement that can dramatically change your space. Here’s a look at the typical steps involved when we tackle a project like this, ensuring safety and a quality finish for homes across the Denver area.

Step 1: Structural Assessment & Planning. The very first thing we do is assess whether the wall is indeed load-bearing. This often involves looking at floor plans or consulting with a structural professional. Once confirmed, we'll plan out the necessary structural support, which almost always includes designing for a new, properly sized header to carry the load above the widened opening. We also identify any potential conflicts with electrical, plumbing, or HVAC lines that might be hidden in the wall.

Step 2: Permit Application & Preparations. If structural changes are involved, obtaining the necessary permits from your local building department is crucial. We can guide you through this process. Concurrently, we prepare the work area, protecting your existing floors and furniture from dust and debris, and setting up temporary supports if required.

Step 3: Demolition & Rough Framing. With preparations complete, we carefully remove the existing framing and any drywall as needed. Then, we install the new structural header and reframe the widened opening according to the approved plans, ensuring everything is plumb, level, and securely fastened.

Step 4: Utility Rerouting (If Needed). If any electrical, plumbing, or HVAC lines were discovered and needed to be moved, this is where we coordinate with qualified sub-trades to reroute them safely and correctly, integrating them into the new wall structure.

Step 5: Finish Work. Once the framing and utilities are in place, we move on to the finish work. This includes installing new drywall, taping, mudding, sanding, and then applying primer and paint. We also install new casing and trim around the widened doorway to seamlessly blend it with your home's existing aesthetic. Sometimes this also means addressing flooring where the old doorway used to be. For complex projects, especially those affecting egress or accessibility, professional help is important.
