Key Steps to Improve Home Mobility for Limited Balance

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

If you or a loved one are experiencing limited balance, making your home safer and easier to navigate is a top priority. Many people wonder where to start and what upgrades will be most effective. The good news is that with some practical steps, you can significantly improve movement through your home.

Step 1: Identify Barriers to Movement. Start by walking through your home, checking all entries, hallways, doors, and stairs. Pay close attention to bathrooms and kitchens, as these areas often present the most challenges. Look for anything that could cause a trip or make movement difficult for someone with a walker, cane, or limited balance. Consider how you access controls and storage.

Step 2: Prioritize Practical Modifications. Once you've identified the barriers, you can begin making practical changes. Widened doorways are often a game-changer, allowing easier passage for mobility aids. Ramps or no-step entries at exterior doors can eliminate dangerous thresholds. Inside, handrails on both sides of staircases are crucial, and improving stair visibility with contrasting treads or better lighting can prevent falls. Accessible switches and outlets, placed at a comfortable height, also make a big difference.

Step 3: Plan for Future Needs and Professional Help. Some mobility-related changes, especially structural ones like door widening or ramp placement, are much easier and less costly to integrate during a planned remodel. Neglecting exterior access or not checking reach ranges from a seated position are common mistakes.

For projects that involve structural layout, safety-critical access, or code-sensitive work, it's really important to bring in a qualified professional.
