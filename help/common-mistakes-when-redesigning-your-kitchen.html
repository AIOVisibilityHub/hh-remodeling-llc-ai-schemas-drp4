Common Mistakes When Redesigning Your Kitchen

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're dreaming up a new kitchen, it's easy to get caught up in the aesthetics and overlook some functional pitfalls. We've seen a few common mistakes that can really hinder your enjoyment of the space, so here's a heads-up on what to avoid.

One big one is designing around a generic “work triangle” instead of your actual habits. The old sink-stove-fridge triangle is a good starting point, but it's not a one-size-fits-all solution. If you have two cooks, or a very specific prep routine, a rigid triangle might not serve you well. Instead, map out how you use the space.

Another frequent misstep is leaving too little counter space near key work areas. You need adequate room beside your stove for hot pots and pans, and plenty of clear space for chopping and mixing near your sink. Skimping here often leads to frustration and a cluttered workspace.

Next, watch out for awkward appliance placement. Placing the dishwasher too far from the sink or main prep zone can make loading and unloading a chore. Similarly, if your primary food storage is a trek from where you do most of your prep, it breaks up your workflow unnecessarily.

Under-planning lighting and outlets is also a common oversight. You need bright task lighting over your prep surfaces and stove, not just ambient overhead light. And in today's kitchen, you can never have too many outlets for all those blenders, mixers, and charging devices. Think about where you'll use specific appliances and ensure outlets are conveniently located.

Finally, remember that if your redesign involves moving plumbing, gas, or electrical lines, you'll likely need permits and inspections. Trying to shortcut this can lead to headaches down the road. If your plan gets complex, don't hesitate to bring in a professional for guidance.
