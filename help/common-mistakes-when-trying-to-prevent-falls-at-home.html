Common Mistakes When Trying to Prevent Falls at Home

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're trying to make your home safer, it's easy to overlook some common pitfalls that can actually reduce the effectiveness of your efforts. Understanding these mistakes can help you create a truly fall-resistant environment. Here are a few to watch out for:

  • Leaving loose rugs or cords in walkways: These are classic trip hazards. How to avoid it: Secure all rugs with non-slip backing or remove them entirely, and tape down or bundle electrical cords away from high-traffic areas.
  • Relying on poor or insufficient lighting: Dim areas hide obstacles and make navigating difficult, especially at night. How to avoid it: Install brighter bulbs, add nightlights in hallways and bathrooms, and ensure all frequently used areas are well-lit.
  • Failing to add grab bars or handrails where needed: Bathrooms and stairs are prime locations for falls due to lack of support. How to avoid it: Install sturdy grab bars in showers, next to toilets, and secure handrails on both sides of staircases.
  • Not addressing unstable furniture: Items that can tip or slide when leaned on offer no real support. How to avoid it: Ensure all furniture is stable and heavy enough to support a person's weight if used for balance.
  • Ignoring clutter in circulation paths: Even small items can be a tripping hazard if they’re in the way. How to avoid it: Keep pathways clear and regularly declutter all rooms, especially those you move through often.

While many simple fixes can be done yourself, if you find yourself or a loved one repeatedly falling, or if you use mobility aids, it's smart to consult with a professional. They can conduct a comprehensive safety assessment and recommend tailored, structural solutions.
