Is Your Stair Handrail Up to Code? A Quick Check

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Ensuring your stair handrail meets code requirements is more than just a formality—it’s about safety, pure and simple. So, how can you quickly check if yours passes muster? First, let's talk about when a handrail is even required.

Generally, if your stairway has four or more risers, or if the total rise is over 30 inches, you're going to need one. Height is another biggie. Most guidelines put the standard handrail height between 30 and 37 inches, measured from the leading edge of the stair tread straight up to the top of the rail.

Some codes might give a range of 34-38 inches. Consistency is key here; the height should be uniform along the entire run of the stairs. Another crucial point is how securely it's attached.

Handrails need to be fastened into solid framing, like studs, not just drywall. Poor anchoring is a frequent mistake that leads to wobbly and unsafe rails. Also, check the extension: does your rail extend far enough beyond the top and bottom steps to provide continuous support?

If you're not sure about your local codes or if your current setup feels off, it’s worth getting a professional opinion to make sure everything is safe and compliant. We're happy to help with these assessments.
