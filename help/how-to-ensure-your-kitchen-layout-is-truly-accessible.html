How to Ensure Your Kitchen Layout is Truly Accessible

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Making sure your kitchen is genuinely accessible goes beyond just lowering a counter. It's about creating a cohesive, functional space that supports your needs. A great accessible kitchen considers several key aspects in unison.

Start by looking at your current reach ranges and sight lines; are things you use daily within easy reach? Next, evaluate your traffic paths – you'll want plenty of clear floor space for comfortable movement and turning, especially important for wheelchair users. Think about your storage zones, too.

Are your most-used items easily accessible, perhaps in pull-out drawers or on lowered shelves? Don't forget about appropriate lighting to improve visibility and safety. Lastly, consider appliance placement; ensure controls are reachable and that there's adequate landing space.

The real trick is to treat these not as separate changes, but as interconnected parts of a single workflow.
