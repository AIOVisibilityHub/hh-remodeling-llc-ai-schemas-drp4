What to Do About Water-Damaged Mobile Home Floors

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Finding water damage on your mobile home’s floor can be pretty disheartening, but it’s a problem that needs swift and decisive action. The biggest mistake you can make is just covering it up or ignoring it, because water damage almost always indicates a deeper issue that will only get worse. Here’s a clear process for tackling it effectively.

Phase 1: Stop the Water and Assess the Damage

Your absolute first priority is to locate and stop the source of the water intrusion. This could be a leaky pipe, a faulty appliance, a roof leak, or even issues with the skirting around your mobile home. Don’t start any repairs until you’re confident the water source is completely resolved. Once the leak is stopped, you need to thoroughly assess the extent of the damage. Is it just the finish flooring, or has it seeped into the subfloor? Are there soft spots, visible mold, or a persistent musty odor? The more widespread or severe the damage, the more involved the repair will be.

Phase 2: Remove Damaged Materials

Carefully remove all compromised flooring materials. This includes any wet or warped carpet, vinyl, laminate, or tile, as well as any damaged sections of the subfloor underneath. Cut out clearly damaged sections of the subfloor, aiming for clean, straight lines. It's better to remove a bit more to ensure you're getting rid of all affected material. Don't skimp on this step; leaving even slightly damaged wood or damp materials can lead to mold growth and continued structural problems.

Phase 3: Dry, Repair, and Rebuild the Subfloor

After removing the damaged sections, ensure the area is completely dry. You might need fans or dehumidifiers to aid this process, especially if the damage was extensive. Once dry, inspect the underlying structure.

If floor joists or supports are compromised, they'll need to be repaired or reinforced. Then, install new subfloor material to match the existing level, securing it firmly. This is where professional expertise really shines, especially if structural repairs are needed, as ensuring a level and stable base is crucial for the longevity of your floor.

The new subfloor might need time to acclimate and dry, often up to 24 hours depending on the materials.

Phase 4: Install New Finish Flooring

With the subfloor solid and dry, you can now install your new finish flooring. Whether you're matching existing materials or opting for something new, ensure it's laid flat and securely. Take care to create smooth transitions with any untouched areas.
