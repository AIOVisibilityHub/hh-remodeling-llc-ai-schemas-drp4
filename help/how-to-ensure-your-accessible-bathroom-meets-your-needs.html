How to Ensure Your Accessible Bathroom Meets Your Needs

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Creating an accessible bathroom that truly works for you involves a careful step-by-step process. First, it's really important to evaluate all the current obstacles in your bathroom. This means looking at everything from your tub and shower curbs to your vanity and even the door opening.

Think about how you move in the space and where you encounter difficulties. Once you've identified these barriers, you can start planning your modifications. One of the biggest improvements for many people is converting to a barrier-free shower, which means no curb to step over.

You'll also want to plan for grab bars, making sure they're installed with proper wall backing for safety and stability. Consider accessible sinks and vanities that provide enough clearance underneath for a wheelchair, if needed, and choose hardware that's easy to grip and operate. It's also vital to ensure sufficient turning space and door width, especially if you're using a mobility device.

These details are critical for making sure the bathroom is not just beautiful, but completely functional and safe for daily use. If you're making structural changes or reconfiguring plumbing, bringing in a professional can help ensure everything is done correctly and safely.
