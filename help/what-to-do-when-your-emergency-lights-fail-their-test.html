What to Do When Your Emergency Lights Fail Their Test

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

So, you did your monthly push-button check or your annual 90-minute discharge test on your emergency lights, and they didn’t perform as expected. It’s definitely a moment for concern, as these lights are critical for safety during power outages. Don't just reset it and hope for the best; a failed test usually signals an underlying issue that needs attention. Getting to the bottom of it quickly is key to ensuring your home stays safe.

Step 1: Inspect the Fixture and Power. Start by visually inspecting the emergency light fixture for any obvious damage. Then, check the circuit breaker to ensure it hasn't tripped, interrupting the power supply. Confirm the fixture is properly receiving power.

Step 2: Test Bulbs and Batteries. For emergency lights, the rechargeable battery is often the first component to fail, especially after 3-5 years. Test the battery; if it’s old or not holding a charge, it likely needs replacement. If it’s a bulb-based unit, try swapping out the bulbs to see if that resolves the issue. Remember, a light might pass a brief test but still fail to stay on for the required 90 minutes if the battery is weak.

Step 3: Look for Wiring Issues. If the bulbs and battery seem fine, the problem could be with loose connections, corroded battery terminals, or even a bad charging circuit within the unit. Sometimes, the internal wiring can be compromised, or an LED driver might be failing. These issues often require a closer look and proper tools for diagnosis.

Step 4: Consider Professional Help. If you’ve gone through these steps and the emergency light still isn't working reliably, it's time to bring in a professional.
