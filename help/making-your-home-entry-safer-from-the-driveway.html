Making Your Home Entry Safer from the Driveway

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Ensuring a safe and easy route from your driveway to your home's entry is especially important, particularly for older adults or anyone with mobility challenges. It's about creating a smooth, secure transition. Here’s how you can approach it:

Phase 1: Initial Assessment

Start by taking a slow, careful walk from your driveway all the way to your front door. Look closely at the entire path. Are there any cracks, uneven slabs, or subtle shifts in elevation? Pay attention to how easy it is to navigate with a walker or wheelchair, if applicable. Note any obstacles, poor lighting, or tricky thresholds that could cause a stumble.

Phase 2: Identify Problem Areas

Mark any level changes that are greater than half an inch – these are definite trip hazards. Check for surface defects like potholes or crumbling edges on your driveway and walkway. Notice if your entry threshold presents a challenge. Poor lighting can hide these issues, so consider the approach both during the day and at night.

Phase 3: Plan Your Improvements

For small cracks and minor unevenness, patching or leveling might be enough. If you have significant elevation changes, a ramp might be necessary, ideally with a maximum slope of 1:12 for accessibility. Adding non-slip treads, sturdy handrails, and comprehensive pathway lighting are also crucial. Make sure the improvements create a clear visual contrast at any edge changes.
