Avoiding Kitchen Clearance Mistakes

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

When you're planning a kitchen remodel, it's easy to overlook crucial clearance details that can make or break your kitchen's functionality. One of the biggest mistakes we see is treating all open space as truly usable. But here's the thing: appliance doors swing out, people walk, and chairs get pulled back. That 'open' space can quickly become a bottleneck if not planned carefully.

Another common slip-up is ignoring appliance door swing. Think about your dishwasher or oven door fully extended. Does it block a main pathway or trap someone in a corner? You need enough clearance for these doors to open without hitting anything or making it impossible to move around.

Placing traffic through the main work zone is another functional flaw. Your primary work triangle (sink, stove, fridge) should ideally be free of through-traffic to prevent accidents and improve efficiency. If people are constantly cutting through the space where you’re prepping food or cooking, it can be a real headache.

Underestimating the space needed behind seating areas, like an island or peninsula, is also a frequent issue. You need more than just enough room for the stool; consider the space for someone to comfortably sit down, pull out their chair, and for another person to walk behind them. This is often an area where a few extra inches can make a huge difference in comfort and flow.

Finally, don't just aim for minimums. While 36 inches is a standard for walkways, and 42-48 inches for work aisles depending on how many cooks you have, think about your family's actual use. If you have multiple people cooking or frequently entertain, lean towards the wider end of those recommendations. To avoid these pitfalls, it's often best to work with a kitchen design professional. They can help you visualize these scenarios and ensure your new layout not only looks good but works perfectly for your daily life.
