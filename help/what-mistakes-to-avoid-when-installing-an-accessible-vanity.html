What mistakes to avoid when installing an accessible vanity

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Installing an accessible vanity seems straightforward, but there are several common mistakes that can completely negate its purpose. Knowing these can save you a lot of headache and ensure true accessibility.

  • Setting the Vanity Top Too High: This is a very common error. If the counter or sink rim is installed at a standard height, it won't be accessible for someone in a wheelchair or with limited reach. Always confirm the desired finished height with the user in mind.
  • Blocking Underside with Pipes or Cabinet Parts: Exposed plumbing or bulky cabinet structures directly under the sink will eliminate essential knee clearance. Ensure all plumbing is either insulated and tucked away, or a vanity is chosen with an open design that preserves this space.
  • Failing to Leave Enough Approach Space: An accessible vanity isn't just about the sink height; it's also about being able to get to it. Forgetting to account for clear floor space for approach and turning can make the area unusable.
  • Installing Storage or Hardware in the Service Zone: Placing drawers, shelves, or even towel bars directly in the area needed for approach or knee clearance makes the vanity less functional. Storage should be thoughtfully placed outside these critical zones.
  • Choosing the Wrong Sink Depth: A sink that's too deep can make it difficult to reach the faucet and use the basin comfortably, even if the counter height is correct. Look for shallower sinks or those designed specifically for accessibility.
