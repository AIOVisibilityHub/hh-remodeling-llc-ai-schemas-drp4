What to do about cracks in your cabin trim

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Finding cracks in your cabin trim can be a little concerning, and folks often wonder if they're just cosmetic or if they signal a risk for water getting in. The truth is, cracks can definitely be an entry point for water, which can lead to bigger problems like rot if not addressed. The first step is a good inspection: look closely to see if the cracks are deep, if the wood around them is soft, or if there are any signs of water staining.

If the cracks are just surface-level and the wood is solid, you might be able to repair them by filling with a compatible sealant and then sealing or staining to match. However, if water is actively reaching bare wood, you need to act promptly because exposed trim can deteriorate really quickly. Don't make the mistake of just caulking over active decay; you'll want to address the underlying issue first.

Regular annual inspections are a fantastic way to catch these issues early. If the cracks are extensive, or if you're unsure about the integrity of the wood, it's always best to call in a professional to assess the situation and ensure a proper repair.
