Before committing your hard-earned money, you need clarity on exact scopes, sub-trade coordination, and allowances. What to Prepare Before You Start

  • Verify whether the bid includes necessary permits and required local municipal inspections.

Even well-planned kitchen remodels can derail if basic prep steps get skipped early in the design stage. Avoiding classic project traps keeps your schedule realistic and helps prevent costly rework once demolition begins.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Changing design plans after cabinets and custom items have already been ordered: Mid-build alterations cause manufacturer delays, storage fees, and restocking penalties.
  • Ordering appliances before confirming precise cabinet opening dimensions: Even a half-inch discrepancy can prevent a range or built-in refrigerator from sliding into position smoothly.
  • Overlooking ventilation and electrical circuit capacities: Installing higher-draw induction cooktops or commercial-grade range hoods often requires upgrading your breaker panel and exterior ducting.
  • Accepting vague contract allowances: Lump-sum fixture budgets without specific product selections almost always lead to surprise out-of-pocket expenses later.
  • Starting demolition prior to securing building permits: Bypassing municipal approvals can halt your remodel and trigger code enforcement fines.

  • Accurate cabinet blueprint drawings signed off by the installer — Ensures all wall dimensions, ceiling heights, and appliance clearances align before manufacturing begins.

  • Material delivery storage space designated in a dry garage or spare room — Keeps custom trim, flooring boxes, and tile pallets protected from site debris and foot traffic.

Before you finalize new cabinet orders or tear into existing walls, checking your space against physical field measurements is essential. Use this practical pre-remodel checklist to catch layout issues early:

  • Measure total wall lengths at three heights — Prevents ordering cabinets that do not fit out-of-plumb walls.
  • Confirm wall framing is non-load-bearing before removing — Avoids compromising structural ceiling and roof loads.
  • Verify appliance rough-in specifications — Ensures range hoods, cooktops, and refrigerators have proper clearance and dedicated circuit capacity.

  • Check exterior venting paths for range hoods — Confirms mechanical ductwork can exhaust outdoors without hitting structural joists.
  • Confirm island walkway clearances of at least 36 to 42 inches — Keeps cabinet doors and appliances usable without creating pinch points.
  • Check local municipal permit submission guidelines — Clarifies if detailed existing versus proposed floor plans are required.
  • Mark dedicated circuit outlet locations along counter walls — Ensures electrical box placement satisfies required spacing.
