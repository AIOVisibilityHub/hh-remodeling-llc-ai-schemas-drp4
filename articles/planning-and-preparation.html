You need a clean, smooth, and level surface for the tile to adhere correctly. Another big one is inaccurate measurements. Measure twice, cut once, as they say, but also make sure you're establishing level reference lines.

When tackling kitchen plumbing upgrades, even seemingly small errors can lead to big headaches. It's really easy to overlook details that a professional would catch, but knowing these common mistakes can help you avoid them. One frequent blunder is incorrect pipe sizing.

Using pipes that are too small can restrict water flow, while pipes that are too large might not maintain proper pressure. Another common issue is poor drain slope; if your drainpipes don't have the correct downward angle, you'll end up with sluggish drainage or even clogs. Loose supply connections are another big one – a connection that isn't fully tightened is a leak waiting to happen, potentially causing significant water damage.

Mismatched shutoff valves can also create problems; ensure your new fixtures are compatible with the existing or newly installed valves. And perhaps the most critical mistake: failing to thoroughly test for leaks before closing up walls or cabinets. Always run water through the system and check every joint.

It's much easier to fix a small drip when everything is exposed. If you're not confident in your plumbing skills, or if your project involves moving lines, it's definitely wise to bring in a professional.
